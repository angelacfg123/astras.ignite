A Silent Archive Wandering the Moon

Apollo 11 landed on the night of July 20 of the year, 1969 with two astronauts, Commander Neil A. Armstrong and LM pilot Edwin E. "Buzz" Aldrin, Jr. The so-called “That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind”, said Armstrong as the first steps on the Moon were made. He proceeds to pick up some samples of lunar materials in a exigency manner. Aldrin said, and I quote called the celestial lunar a "magnificent desolation”. The two astronauts then deployed EASEP or the Early Apollo Surface Experiments Package, which later on will be deserted permanently on the Moon. Yet, that’s not the only thing that will be left. The EASEP, the kick starter for all of the other Apollo missions, tells us stories- adventures of the activities and conditions of the lunar beauty, to Earth.

After travelling a distance of 250 meters, the first men of the two, Aldrin returned to the Lunar Module first, then Armstrong followed him 12 minutes later, after spending almost 1 hour and 41 minutes on the moon’s surface. The LM (Lunar Module) lifted off from the Moon, carrying the two astronauts back on their journey to home, on the evening of 21 July 1969. When the spacecraft departed, the descent stage was left behind. No, not because it was no longer useful to them, but quite the opposite. The descent stage served a purpose as a launching pad for the ascent stage, making sure those astronauts came back. It was holding up the lower part of the spacecraft, and contained the landing rocket, and other vital parts of the spacecraft. So on, the fate of the following descent stages of other Apollo missions to the mean follows the same story.

Back to Earth, the spacecraft has just docked with the CSM, at night. The LM was ejected back to lunar orbit on the dawn of 22 July. The whereabouts of the module was unknown. We still don’t know the tragic demise of this. Some say it crashed into the lunar surface around 1 to 4 months after its disappearance. Farewell, LM of Apollo 11, we barely knew you.

September 12, 1959, marked a special milestone for the Soviet Union. It had become the first human-made object to make an impact on the Moon, our Moon with Luna 2! Truth to be told, that this achievement came a long way. Entering the Space Age, or the United States and Soviet Union call it, both of them set their aim on the Moon as a focus for exploration with robotic spacecraft. In the March of 1958, The Soviet Union approved its first ever development for a spacecraft to interact with the celestial lunar. That same year, the Soviet Union launched three attempts but ended with launch failures. On January 2, 1959, the Soviet Union announced the launch of their first Cosmic Rocket, which was renamed to Luna 1 in 1963. However, it missed the moon by 4,000 miles, failing the mission. Even though the failure, it had achieved to be the first ever spacecraft to enter solar orbit.

Following the year 1959, another Soviet Union’s launch failure, they still continued their ambitious goals. Finally on September 2, their Second Cosmic Rocket successfully achieved the next step of their mission. Second Cosmic Rocket, or Luna 2 escaped velocity and placed the spacecraft, virtually identical to Luna 1, on an intercept course with the Moon. Thus, on the second day, 13th September, it became the first spacecraft to make contact with another celestial body when it impacted the Moon between Mare Imbrium and Mare Serenitatis, about 160 miles from where Apollo 15 would land 12 years later. This extraordinary spacecraft, came less than two years after the first artificial satellite was sent into orbit, highlighting rapid advancements in space technology during the early Cold War era. Luna 2's journey lasted 36 hours before it impacted the Moon at a speed of approximately 3 kilometers per second, landing near the craters Archimedes, Aristillus, and Autolycus. This mission became the blueprint for data about cosmic rays, magnetic fields, and micrometeorite impacts, revealing that the Moon lacked a magnetic field and providing insights into Earth's radiation belts. Furthermore, Luna 2 sets blue prints for future missions, showcasing the ability to navigate and aim spacecraft accurately, a fundamental requirement for subsequent explorations. Oh, Luna 2, you are always and will be the symbol of human creation in reaching another celestial body.
